# Final Review: The 30-Day Countdown

A template, not a fixed script — swap in whichever of this site's
domains actually matches your target role before following it. The
structure (foundations → systems → specialization → final rehearsal)
is what matters; the specific pages you plug into each week should
track the actual job description you're preparing for.

## Week 1: Foundation Review

**Goal:** make sure the fundamentals you'll be judged on fastest —
language/backend core knowledge and DSA — are genuinely solid, not
just familiar.

- **Language/backend fundamentals** — whichever of [Backend >
  Python](../backend/python/pythons-execution-model.md),
  [Go](../backend/go/go-fundamentals.md), or
  [Kotlin](../backend/kotlin/kotlin-for-jvm-interviews.md) matches the
  role, reviewed cold, not just skimmed.
- **DSA practice** — a daily block working through
  [Algorithms](../dsa/big-o-and-memory-hardware.md), starting from
  whichever pattern category you're weakest on, not alphabetically.
- **Resume and portfolio polish** — apply
  [Resume, GitHub & Case Study Optimization](resume-github-and-case-study-optimization.md)
  now, early, so the rest of the month's prep can reference a resume
  that's already in its near-final state.
- **Company and role research** — read the actual job description
  closely enough to identify which 2-3 of this site's domains are
  genuinely load-bearing for it — this is what Week 3's specialization
  pulls from.

## Week 2: Systems & Architecture

**Goal:** the system-design and architecture material that shows up in
most senior loops regardless of specific role.

- [System Design Methodology](../architecture/system-design-methodology.md)
  and [Common System Design Patterns](../architecture/system-design-patterns.md) —
  the actual framework for working through a design question live, not
  just case studies memorized in isolation.
- [Scalability Case Studies](../architecture/scalability-case-studies.md) —
  CAP/PACELC and worked examples, a near-universal interview topic.
- The database layer relevant to the target role — [Database Types: The
  Decision Framework](../database/database-types-overview.md) as the
  map, then the specific deep-dive (Postgres, MongoDB, a specialized
  store) the role actually needs.
- Cloud/DevOps fundamentals proportional to the role — not every role
  needs deep AWS knowledge, but [AWS Accounts &
  IAM](../cloud-devops/aws-accounts-and-iam.md) and
  [DevOps Principles & Delivery](../cloud-devops/devops-principles-and-delivery.md)
  are close to universal baseline knowledge today.

## Week 3: Specialization Focus

**Goal:** the 2-3 domains Week 1's research identified as genuinely
specific to this role, gone deep rather than broad.

- If the role is AI/LLM-heavy: the [AI & LLM](../ai-llm/agi-and-asi.md)
  domain, likely starting from
  [How LLMs Actually Work](../ai-llm/how-llms-actually-work.md) through
  whichever of RAG, agents, or evaluation the role actually touches.
- If the role has real testing/QA ownership: [Software
  Testing](../testing/testing-strategy-pyramid-and-trophy.md), from
  the fundamentals through whichever advanced techniques (contract
  testing, mutation testing) the role's maturity level actually uses.
- If the role has real security ownership: [Cyber
  Security](../security/cybersecurity-for-beginners-roadmap.md),
  particularly [The OWASP Top 10](../security/owasp-top-10-in-depth.md)
  and whichever track (offensive, defensive, GRC) the role leans toward.
- If the role is frontend-heavy: the [Frontend](../frontend/frontend-fundamentals.md)
  domain, matched to the actual framework the role uses.
- **Behavioral story refinement begins here, not Week 4** — by this
  point you have enough real, recent technical prep to pull fresh
  examples from when building out
  [Behavioral Interview Prep](behavioral-interview-prep.md)'s story
  bank, rather than reaching for stale, over-used examples.

## Week 4: Final Preparation

**Goal:** rehearsal under real time pressure, not new material — this
week is for surfacing and fixing weak spots, not learning anything for
the first time.

- Work through [Take-Home & Live-Coding
  Exercises](take-home-and-live-coding-exercises.md) cold, under a real
  time box, exactly as described there.
- Schedule at least two full mock interviews — one technical, one
  behavioral — timeboxed like the real thing, ideally recorded for
  self-review on pacing and clarity, not just content.
- Revisit [Validating Your Skill Level](validating-your-skill-level.md)
  — by this point in the month you should have something concrete to
  point to, not just a self-assessment.
- Prepare your own questions for interviewers, and review
  [Interviewing in the AI Era](interviewing-in-the-ai-era.md) if the
  process includes an AI-assisted coding round or an AI-run interview
  stage.

## Daily Rhythm Template

- **Morning** — coding/DSA practice (1-2 hours), while focus is
  freshest.
- **Afternoon** — system design or domain-specific reading (1-2 hours).
- **Evening** — behavioral prep or project/portfolio work (1 hour).
- **Weekly** — at least one mock interview or peer review session,
  every week, not just saved for Week 4.

## The One Piece of Advice That Actually Matters Most

**Consistency over intensity.** A steady daily rhythm across 30 days
reliably beats a handful of exhausting cram sessions — tired, rushed
preparation produces worse retention and worse interview performance
than the same total hours spread out across the full month.
